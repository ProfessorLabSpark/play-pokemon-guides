# Pokémon TCG Judge & Tournament Organizer Community Guideline (Proposed)

**Proposed by:** Bryan Crisler Jr.  
**Status:** Draft — seeking review and input from experienced Professors before broader circulation  
**Version:** 2.1  
**Purpose:** A proposed compensation, staffing, and compliance guideline that stores and Leagues could voluntarily adopt. This is not an official Play! Pokémon document and carries no authority beyond what the community chooses to give it. It's offered as a starting point for discussion — individual store agreements would reference this document rather than re-negotiating terms from scratch each time, once (and if) the community has weighed in and the content reflects broad agreement rather than one person's proposal.

---

## Scope

This guideline applies to Play! Pokémon league-level sanctioned events (League Challenges, League Cups, and similar league-tier events) where a store owner or organizer requests the presence of one or more Pokémon Professors certified to fulfill the role of Organizer, Judge, and/or Scorekeeper, and who are not employed by the hosting store.

This guideline does not apply to:

- Regional Championships, International Championships, or the World Championships, which have their own established staffing and compensation structures.
- Store staff who are certified Professors running their own store's events.
- Any event where these roles are handled internally by store employees rather than an independently requested outside Professor.

This guideline exists to give organizers and independently requested Professors a shared, predictable baseline for league-level events specifically, where no formal compensation standard currently exists.

---

## 1. Why This Guideline Is Being Proposed

Judges and Tournament Organizers are often compensated inconsistently — sometimes generously, sometimes not at all, frequently without any advance agreement on what "today" will pay. This creates two problems:

- **For judges:** Uncertainty about compensation discourages reliable participation and makes it hard to commit time confidently.
- **For stores:** Without a shared reference point, every event becomes a fresh negotiation, and compliance requirements (TO presence, certified scorekeeping, deck checks) get treated as optional rather than structural.

This is an early draft attempting to address both by sketching a **default compensation model that works even for stores with little or no product/credit infrastructure**, scaling as the store grows, and tying directly to Play! Pokémon compliance requirements — so judges know what to expect, and stores know what it costs.

**Core principle behind the proposal:** Compensation and cost should both be knowable before the event starts — a judge shouldn't have to wonder "will I get paid today, and how much?" and a store shouldn't have to wonder what staffing an event will cost.

**This document is not yet a community-vetted guideline.** It's a proposal that needs review from more experienced Professors, TOs, and judges before it should be treated as representative of community consensus. Feedback, corrections, and disagreement are welcome — the goal is for the content to reflect what the community actually agrees on, not one person's opinion presented as settled practice.

## 2. Play! Pokémon Compliance Requirements Summary

- A **Tournament Organizer (TO)** must be present for the entire event.
- Events must be sanctioned **at least two weeks** prior to the event date. *(Source: [Sanctioning and Reporting League Cups and Challenges](https://support.play.pokemon.com/hc/en-us/articles/34822239548820-Sanctioning-and-Reporting-League-Cups-and-Challenges), Play! Pokémon Support.)*
- **Store owners** who are not Professor-certified must source a certified Organizer to run sanctioned tournament events on their behalf — Organizer certification is not automatic for store owners.
- **Anyone serving as TO** (owner or otherwise) must hold Organizer certification through the Play! Pokémon Professor Program. Per official guidance: *"The person planning to sanction a League Cup or League Challenge must be a Professor with a valid Organizer certification."* Ownership alone does not confer eligibility. *(Source: [Sanctioning and Reporting League Cups and Challenges](https://support.play.pokemon.com/hc/en-us/articles/34822239548820-Sanctioning-and-Reporting-League-Cups-and-Challenges), Play! Pokémon Support, updated Oct 28, 2025.)*
- **Scorekeeping** is handled by the TO by default. Per the Tournament Rules Handbook: *"The Scorekeeper is a tournament staff member who is responsible for the accurate recording of match results and generation of pairings as a tournament progresses on behalf of the Organizer. The Scorekeeper role is one that can be fulfilled by a Professor, Judge, the Tournament Organizer, or another person who is tasked specifically with the role, but this person must hold the appropriate certification to operate tournament software."* The Handbook doesn't name the specific certification, but the TOM End User License Agreement restricts use of the software to organizing tournaments compliant with Pokémon's Organizer guidelines, and Play! Pokémon Regional Professor Coordinators have consistently confirmed in the official Professor community that "the appropriate certification" means an **active Organizer certification** specifically — TOM cannot be downloaded or operated without one, regardless of other Professor Program activity. *(Sources: [Tournament Rules Handbook](https://www.pokemon.com/static-assets/content-assets/cms2/pdf/play-pokemon/rules/play-pokemon-tournament-rules-handbook-en.pdf) §3.3.5, last revised May 21, 2026; TOM End User License Agreement; Play! Pokémon Professor Discord — see Sources section for full citations.)*
- **Championship Series events** (Challenges, Cups) do not strictly require a judge — per the official guide: *"The only required role to run a League Challenge or League Cup is an Organizer with the appropriate Professor roles... While a Judge is not required at any League event, it is highly recommended that stores have a Judge present at the event to help support the integrity of the tournament."* This proposal's suggestion to staff judges at these events is a recommendation this guideline agrees with, not a restatement of a strict Play! Pokémon requirement. *(Source: [League Challenges, League Cups, and Prerelease Guide](https://www.pokemon.com/static-assets/content-assets/cms2/pdf/play-pokemon/rules/play-pokemon-league-challenges-cups-and-prerelease-guide-en.pdf), §6.2, The Pokémon Company International, last revision April 7, 2026.)*
- **Rulings, deck checks, and penalties** must be handled exclusively by certified Professors.
- Results must be reported within **seven days** of the tournament's conclusion. Late reporting is considered delinquent and may jeopardize eligibility to host future Premier events. *(Source: [Sanctioning and Reporting League Cups and Challenges](https://support.play.pokemon.com/hc/en-us/articles/34822239548820-Sanctioning-and-Reporting-League-Cups-and-Challenges), Play! Pokémon Support.)*
- **Deck checks** are required for a minimum of 10% of participants at Championship Series events.
- **Tournament Operations Manager (TOM)** use is mandatory for League Challenges and League Cups — third-party software or manual/by-hand running of these events is not permitted, and doing so may invalidate the event. *(Source: [Sanctioning and Reporting League Cups and Challenges](https://support.play.pokemon.com/hc/en-us/articles/34822239548820-Sanctioning-and-Reporting-League-Cups-and-Challenges); [Tournament Operations Manager (TOM) FAQ](https://support.play.pokemon.com/hc/en-us/articles/27119497000724-Tournament-Operations-Manager-TOM-FAQ), Play! Pokémon Support.)*
- TOM is provided to official Pokémon Professors specifically to operate sanctioned tournaments, and whoever sanctions an event must have been confirmed by the League Owner as having permission to do so for that league. The same Organizer should sanction the tournament in Play! Tools and run the event in TOM to avoid system errors. *(Sources: [TOM Operator Guide](https://assets.pokemon.com/tournament_software/tom_guide.pdf), The Pokémon Company International; [Running a Prerelease Event](https://support.play.pokemon.com/hc/en-us/articles/33930103387796-Running-a-Prerelease-Event), Play! Pokémon Support.)*

Compensation is entirely at the store's discretion — TPCi does not require judges to be paid. But **compliance is not discretionary**, and this guideline treats the two as separate tracks: pay is negotiable, presence and certification are not.

## 3. Compensation Model: Product-First, Scalable

Most independent stores — especially new ones — don't have a store credit system or excess product to spare. This proposal defaults to a **pack-based hourly rate**, which works even with minimal inventory, and scales naturally as the store grows into cash or credit options.

### Baseline Rate: Role Compression, Not Role Tiers

None of these roles are professional-level positions, and compensation shouldn't be structured as if they were. Rather than paying different rates for Floor Judge vs. Head Judge vs. TO as if they were a hierarchy of skill, this proposal suggests compensating based on **how many roles a person is covering** at once — since that's what actually changes the workload and responsibility during an event.

| Coverage | Rate (packs/hour) | Notes |
| -------- | ------------------ | ----- |
| One role (Floor Judge, Head Judge, or TO — including TO's default scorekeeping duty) | 2 packs/hr | Baseline rate. TO handling scorekeeping is not compression — that's the TO's default responsibility per Section 4, not an added role. |
| Two or more roles beyond that default (e.g., TO + Head Judge actively on the floor, Head Judge + Floor Judge coverage) | 2.5 packs/hr | Reflects genuinely added responsibility beyond a single role's normal scope, not a "higher tier" role |
| Judge Trainee | Volunteer, unpaid | Mentorship-based, no compensation expected |

**Rounding:** Round up to the nearest whole pack at the end of the event (e.g., 3.5 hours at 2 packs/hr = 7 packs, not 7.5; 4 hours at 2.5 packs/hr = 10 packs).

### What This Actually Costs the Store

Packs/hour is the judge-facing number, but the store-facing number is dollars — and the honest way to get there is the store's **own per-pack cost from its own invoice** (case/box cost ÷ packs per box), not a guessed or generic figure and not MSRP. Per-pack cost varies by set, distributor, and order volume, and this proposal has no reliable basis for asserting a specific number — a store owner should pull it from their own distributor pricing, not from anything stated here.

Formula: **packs/hr × store's actual per-pack cost = real hourly cost.** Multiply by hours worked for the per-event total. Worth doing this math once per role tier so the actual dollar exposure is known before adopting the rate table above, rather than reasoning about it in the abstract as "just a couple packs."

**Which cost basis is the honest one depends on whether that pack would have sold anyway.** Given current demand for the product, this proposal assumes it would have — booster packs are a hot commodity right now, moving fast enough that giving one away is a real forgone sale, not just stock sitting on a shelf. On that assumption, the more honest cost basis is closer to **retail price**, not wholesale cost. That assumption can flip if demand cools or a store is sitting on slower-moving older product — a store owner who knows their own sell-through rate is better positioned to judge the right number for their situation than any fixed rule this proposal could state.

**A caution about this model:** Role compression is genuinely cost-efficient, but it also creates an incentive to understaff events — one person covering three functions is cheaper than three people covering one each, regardless of whether the event actually needs that many hands. This proposal doesn't treat that as a reason to avoid the model, but stores using it should weigh it against Play! Pokémon's own stated position on staffing:

> "The only required role to run a League Challenge or League Cup is an Organizer with the appropriate Professor roles... While a Judge is not required at any League event, it is highly recommended that stores have a Judge present at the event to help support the integrity of the tournament."

Pokémon does not mandate compensation levels or staffing counts, and is explicit about that:

> "Pokémon makes no recommendation regarding entry fees beyond the stipulation that Play! Pokémon Store Owners must follow all local legal requirements that may apply when deciding if, how, and what to charge."

*(Sources: [League Challenges, League Cups, and Prerelease Guide](https://www.pokemon.com/static-assets/content-assets/cms2/pdf/play-pokemon/rules/play-pokemon-league-challenges-cups-and-prerelease-guide-en.pdf) §6.2; [Play! Pokémon Store Handbook](https://www.pokemon.com/static-assets/content-assets/cms2/pdf/play-pokemon/rules/play-pokemon-store-handbook-en.pdf) §5.1.1; both The Pokémon Company International, current as of April/May 2026.)*

In short: cheaper staffing (via role compression) is in a store's short-term financial interest, but adequate staffing — even at a compressed rate per person — is in the store's actual interest in running events players want to return to. This proposal's suggestion is to use role compression to make paying multiple people affordable, not as a justification for running events understaffed to save cost.

**A clarifying note on what "packs" means in this model:** Throughout this proposal, "packs" refers to the store's own retail booster pack inventory — ordinary stock the store purchases through its normal channels — not the promotional materials Play! Pokémon ships to stores quarterly. Those quarterly shipments (including Prize Packs) are promotional/marketing material, not prizing in the competitive sense — their stated purpose is *"promoting player attendance at Play! Pokémon events,"* and the Store Handbook gives the store owner real discretion over how to distribute them to reward participation. That discretion has one boundary relevant here, and it's narrower than it might first appear: *"These items can be given away as participation rewards but are not compensation for Professors or League Assistants (with the exception of expansion-specific staff promo cards)."* Read carefully, this doesn't bar judges or Organizers from ever receiving these materials — a judge who also participates, or who's simply included in a goodwill giveaway like any other community member, can still receive them. What it rules out is *structuring* the promotional shipment as the payment for their staffing work — e.g., "you get 8 packs for judging tonight" framed as compensation. The distinction is about framing and intent, not about judges being excluded from participation rewards generally. None of this restricts a store's own purchased inventory either way — compensating judges from regular retail stock, structured explicitly as pay, is simply a business decision, the same as an employee discount would be. The one built-in exception on the Play!-provided side is Prerelease Kits, which explicitly include a booster display designated *"for prizing and Judge support"* — so Prerelease packs specifically may be used for judge compensation because Play! Pokémon designed them for that purpose. *(Source: [Play! Pokémon Store Handbook](https://www.pokemon.com/static-assets/content-assets/cms2/pdf/play-pokemon/rules/play-pokemon-store-handbook-en.pdf) §4.1–4.3; [League Challenges, League Cups, and Prerelease Guide](https://www.pokemon.com/static-assets/content-assets/cms2/pdf/play-pokemon/rules/play-pokemon-league-challenges-cups-and-prerelease-guide-en.pdf) §3.2; both The Pokémon Company International.)*

### Scaling Up: When Product or Credit Becomes Available

As a store grows, it may offer equivalent value in other forms. The **pack rate is the reference point** — other compensation should be priced against it, not negotiated separately each time.

| Store's Capability | Equivalent Offer |
| ------------------- | ----------------- |
| Some product, no credit system | Packs (per table above), or IOU for next event if temporarily out of stock |
| Product + informal credit | Packs, cash, or store credit (valued at the store's own cost or MSRP per pack — not an arbitrary figure) |
| Established credit system | Judge's choice: packs, cash, or credit (+10-20% bonus for credit) |

**Example:** If a store later wants to offer store credit instead of packs, that should be priced against the packs/hour rate, not treated as an unrelated new number. Use **the store's own cost or current MSRP per pack** as the conversion rate — not a fixed assumption like "$4/pack" — so that if packs become available again, the credit could actually purchase the same number of packs 1:1. This keeps the reference point consistent even as payment format changes, and even as pack prices themselves change over time.

### When There's No Product At All

If a store genuinely has nothing to offer (true startup phase), the event should be run as one of the following — **decided and communicated in advance**, never assumed after the fact:

1. **Volunteer/unpaid**, clearly labeled as such and agreed to by the judge beforehand, or
2. **Deferred compensation (IOU):** judge is owed packs once the store restocks, tracked in writing, or
3. **Free/casual play session** (not a sanctioned tournament) — avoids the compensation question entirely and avoids compliance obligations tied to sanctioned events. This is arguably **the best option for a brand-new store**, not just a fallback: without the formal weight of a sanctioned tournament, judges and organizers can show up as community members rather than staff — shorter time commitment, lower pressure, and a genuinely casual environment for everyone. It's a way to build community and player relationships early, before the store has the product or infrastructure to formally compensate anyone, without asking judges to work in an unpaid staffing capacity. A new store can grow into sanctioned events (and the compensation model above) once it has the volume and product to support them.

**The scenario this proposal is trying to prevent:** a judge performing full TO/Head Judge duties under the assumption of one compensation model, then being paid under a different, lower one after the fact with no advance notice — a mismatch that costs the store trust and future coverage just as much as it costs the judge pay they expected. Compensation terms should be settled and communicated **at or before the event is announced** — not "before the event" in some loose sense, and certainly not negotiated in person when the judge arrives to work. Securing judging staff for an event isn't complete until a compensation agreement is actually reached — the two aren't separable tasks. Lining up a judge's availability without settling what they'll be paid isn't really staffing the event yet; it's leaving an open question that shouldn't still be open once the judge shows up to work.

## 4. Role & Certification Requirements

### A Note on Compensation and Taxes

Compensation received for judging or organizing — whether cash, store credit, or product — may be subject to income tax depending on individual circumstances and jurisdiction. This guideline does not attempt to address that question; anyone with concerns about how their compensation should be reported or taxed should consult a qualified tax professional rather than rely on anything stated here.

### Store Owner / League Owner

- Can host casual, unsanctioned Play! Sessions without any certification.
- To run **sanctioned tournament events** (League Challenge, League Cup) as TO, the owner must either hold Organizer certification themselves or source a certified Organizer to run them. Per the Store Handbook: *"Play! Pokémon Store Owners can choose to become a Pokémon Professor and go on to gain an Organizer certification, which will allow them to run and report their own tournaments. If a Play! Pokémon Store Owner chooses not to become a Pokémon Professor and obtain an Organizer certification, they will need to source an Organizer to run tournament events for them."* Whoever that Organizer is must also be formally invited as a League Assistant for that specific store. *(Source: [Play! Pokémon Store Handbook](https://www.pokemon.com/static-assets/content-assets/cms2/pdf/play-pokemon/rules/play-pokemon-store-handbook-en.pdf) §3.2.3.1, The Pokémon Company International, last revision May 21, 2026.)*
- Responsibilities: venue, sanctioning, compliance oversight, community standards.

### Tournament Organizer (TO)

- Must hold Organizer certification through the Play! Pokémon Professor Program.
- **Must be physically present for the entire event.**
- Responsibilities: sanctioning, reporting, compliance, logistics, scorekeeping (by default).

**TO Change (within two weeks of event):**

- Backup TO may be designated.
- Support ticket must be opened with TPCi to switch the TO.
- Players must be notified of the change and warned the event's sanctioning is pending approval.
- If no certified backup is available, the event must be cancelled and players notified immediately.

**TO Change (more than two weeks out):** Cancel/reschedule and recreate the event under the new TO in Play! Tools.

### Scorekeeper

- TO by default, or another tournament staff member — Professor, Judge, or otherwise — tasked specifically with the role.
- Whoever fills this role must hold the certification required to operate tournament software (TOM), which in practice means an **active Organizer certification**. *(See Section 2 sourcing: Tournament Rules Handbook §3.3.5.)*
- Required whenever the TO is actively judging rather than stationed at scoring.
- **Related but distinct — League Assistant permission tiers in Play! Tools:** The Store Handbook defines three permission levels for League Assistants, which govern what a staff member can do in Play! Tools regardless of Professor status: **Basic** (view store pages, report/edit Play! Session attendance, cannot create sessions), **Enhanced** (adds ability to create Play! Sessions; if also Organizer-certified, can sanction/report Friendly Tournaments), and **Specialist** (adds the ability, if also Organizer-certified, to sanction Friendly Tournaments, League Challenges, League Cups, and Prereleases). Critically, permission level alone does not substitute for certification: *"If a League Assistant is granted specialist permissions by a Play! Pokémon Store Owner before they meet the requirements for those permissions, they will still not be able to sanction or report League Challenges, League Cups, or Prereleases until they have acquired the necessary certifications."* This confirms Organizer certification is the actual gate for sanctioning and TOM use, not the League Assistant permission tier by itself. *(Source: [Play! Pokémon Store Handbook](https://www.pokemon.com/static-assets/content-assets/cms2/pdf/play-pokemon/rules/play-pokemon-store-handbook-en.pdf) §3.2.3.2, The Pokémon Company International, last revision May 21, 2026.)*
- **Note:** This proposal includes a suggestion (see Section 10) that Play! Pokémon consider allowing League Assistants to fill this role for League-level events specifically, without requiring full Organizer certification. As of this draft, that is not current policy — the requirement above reflects the rules as they currently stand.

### Head Judge

- Certifications required: TCG Judge, Deck Check, Advanced Judging Skills.
- Responsibilities: rulings, appeals, penalties, floor judge oversight.

### Floor Judge

- Certifications required: TCG Judge, Deck Check.
- Responsibilities: match rulings, deck checks, assisting Head Judge.

### Judge Trainee

- Volunteer, unpaid. Mentorship track toward certification.

### Combined Roles (TO + Head Judge)

A single certified individual can hold both TO and Head Judge responsibilities — common and efficient for smaller stores, and a reasonable fallback when the store owner (if serving as TO) is unavailable. Per Section 3, this combined coverage is compensated at the **2.5 packs/hour** rate rather than a separate rate per role.

**Requirement:** If the TO/Head Judge is actively walking the floor and conducting deck checks, a **separate scorekeeper holding the certification required to operate TOM** (in practice, an active Organizer certification) is needed at the scoring station. Per the Tournament Rules Handbook, this role can be filled by a Professor, Judge, the TO, or another person tasked specifically with it — but whoever it is must hold that certification under current policy. Uncertified staff cannot fill this role today.

| Event Size | Suggested Setup |
| ---------- | ---------------- |
| Under 16 players | Solo TO/Judge/Scorekeeper acceptable |
| 16–24 players | TO/Head Judge (floor) + certified scorekeeper |
| 24–32 players | TO/Head Judge + Floor Judge + certified scorekeeper |
| 32+ players | TO/Head Judge + multiple Floor Judges + certified scorekeeper |

**Backup TO coverage:** If the store owner is the only certified TO, that is a **single point of failure** for compliance. When the owner is unavailable and no certified backup exists, the event either can't run as a sanctioned tournament, or runs non-compliantly (e.g., uncertified staff operating TOM under the owner's login — this does not satisfy either the presence requirement or the certification requirement). Certifying at least one regular judge as TO closes this gap at no additional cost, since that judge is typically present anyway.

## 5. Prize Support Structure (Suggested)

### Prizing Is Calculated Per Division, Not Per Event

TOM treats Juniors, Seniors, and Masters as separate divisions for standings and prizing, even when they're paired together in one pod. This means the prize pool must be split by division **before** any percentage model is applied — a single combined pool for the whole event doesn't match how TOM actually prizes, and treating it as one pool risks a payout structure that's effectively age-based (not allowed; see Best Practices below).

**Example (24 players):**

| Division | Players | Prize Pool (2 packs/player) |
| -------- | ------- | ---------------------------- |
| Masters | 2 | 4 packs |
| Seniors | 8 | 16 packs |
| Juniors | 14 | 28 packs |

Each division's pool is then distributed within that division using one of the models below.

### Suggested Allocation

Where an entry fee is charged, the store converts it into a pack pool at a chosen **packs-per-player** rate (per division, per above) — e.g. 1 or 2 packs/player. **100% of that pool goes to prizing**; there's no separate percentage carved off the top. The packs-per-player rate is the actual lever, and it determines which distribution method (below) is available. League Nights use the same models, sized to each division's headcount. This is a suggested starting point, not a Play! Pokémon requirement — stores set their own prize support.

### Prize Distribution Methods

| Method | Minimum Pool | How It Works |
| ------ | ------------ | ------------- |
| Participation with Top Cut | 2 packs/player | Every player gets 1 guaranteed pack; remaining pool goes to top finishers. Needs the 2-pack floor so there's still something left to pay out a top cut after every player's guaranteed pack. |
| Just Top Cut | 1 pack/player | Whole pool goes to top finishers, no guaranteed pack for everyone. |
| Pack per Win | 2 packs/player | 1 pack awarded per match win, byes typically counting as a win. No top-cut structure — see below. |

All three are fine as long as the method is announced before Round 1, applied consistently within the division, and **not based on age** — the same method and payout structure must apply to Juniors, Seniors, and Masters alike, even though their pool sizes differ.

### Working Out Pack Payouts: Use the Calculator

Don't hand-build the pack-by-place breakdown for Just Top Cut or Participation with Top Cut — use [Lewymd's prize payout calculator](https://lewymd.com/PrizeCalc/). It covers both methods directly: give it player count, packs/player, and whether every player gets at least one pack, and it returns the pack-by-place table. It's listed as a community resource on [judgeball.com](https://www.judgeball.com/guidebook/community-resources/), which notes it's "by no means the only prizing structure that can be used, but one that has been popular among players."

League Nights use the calculator the same way, sized to the division's headcount — there's no separate "casual" structure.

### More on Pack per Win

Instead of a top-cut structure, Pack per Win rewards each match win directly — arguably more equitable than a top-cut model, since it tracks performance across the whole field instead of concentrating prizes in the top few finishers, so a player who goes 3-2 still walks away with something. Works well for League Nights specifically. Note the pool must cover total wins in the division (roughly half of all matches played, byes included), which is why it needs the same 2-packs/player floor as Participation with Top Cut.

### Distribution Format (Flexible by Product Availability)

The **value** of the prize pool stays fixed; the **format** flexes to what's on hand:

- **Packs only** — when supply is good
- **Packs + store credit** — when packs are limited
- **Store credit only** — when packs are scarce
- **Free/casual events** (no entry fee) — skip formal prize pool structure; use door prizes or promos as available

### Best Practices

- Announce the prize structure, including which distribution method is in use, before Round 1 begins.
- Calculate and announce each division's pool separately — don't present one combined number for a mixed-division event.
- Show the calculation openly (e.g., "14 Juniors × 2 packs/player = 28-pack pool").
- Apply the same method and payout structure across all divisions — never a richer or thinner structure for one age group than another.
- Distribute all official kit contents (promos, mats) per Play! Pokémon guidelines — these are separate from the store's own prize pool contribution.
- Keep a simple written record of what was distributed, to whom, and how.

## 7. Risk Mitigation (Rationale for the Proposal)

### Risk: Compensation model changes without notice to the judge

**Suggested mitigation:** Store Addendum (Section 6) as the source of truth. Any change updated there and communicated before the next event, not discovered at prizing.

### Risk: TO presence or certification requirement not met (staff running TOM under owner's login)

**Impact:** Non-compliant sanctioning, risk to League status, misrepresents who administered the event, and violates the requirement that whoever sanctions must be Organizer-certified and confirmed by the League Owner.  
**Suggested mitigation:** Certify at least one backup TO among regular judges. No sanctioned event should run without a certified, permissioned TO physically present.

### Risk: Inconsistent or unclear prize support

**Impact:** Players feel shorted relative to entry fees paid; damages trust and regional reputation.  
**Suggested mitigation:** Fixed packs-per-player rate and distribution method, announced and shown before the event, regardless of who is running it.

### Risk: Store has no product/credit to offer

**Suggested mitigation:** Decide and communicate the model in advance — volunteer, deferred/IOU, or free casual session. Never resolved after the fact during prizing.

### Risk: Judge takes on unplanned duties beyond judging (e.g., demos, rep visits)

**Suggested mitigation:** Time spent on store-requested tasks outside normal judging still counts toward hourly compensation for that shift, unless otherwise agreed in advance.

## 8. Why Consistency Would Help (Store Benefits, If Adopted)

- **Judge reliability:** Judges who know they'll be paid consistently are far more likely to say yes when asked, and to prioritize a store's events over others.
- **Reduced administrative friction:** No renegotiating terms every event — the Addendum answers it.
- **Compliance protection:** Explicitly tying TO presence and certification to a shared reference reduces risk of sanctioning violations.
- **Scales with the store:** Could work identically for a brand-new store with zero product and an established store with a full credit system — only the numbers in the Addendum change.
- **Builds a reputation among judges:** In a small regional community, word gets around about which stores honor clear compensation terms and which don't. A store known for consistency attracts better and more reliable coverage than one known for ad hoc arrangements.

## 9. Path to Becoming an Actual Community Guideline

This document is currently a **personal proposal**, not a vetted community resource. Before it should be treated as representing broader Professor/judge/TO consensus, it needs:

1. **Review by experienced Professors, TOs, and judges** — particularly anyone who has run League Cups/Challenges regularly, to sanity-check the staffing ratios and compensation model against real-world experience.
2. **Correction of anything inaccurate or outdated** — Play! Pokémon policy changes over time (see Section 2 sourcing dates), and this document will need periodic revisiting even after initial review.
3. **Open disagreement welcomed** — particularly on the compensation numbers (packs/hour rates) and prize pool packs-per-player rate, which are proposed starting points, not verified best practices.
4. **A decision on where this should live** if the community finds it useful — e.g., a shared repository, a pinned Discord resource, or something else — once it's no longer just a single author's draft.

**If you're a store owner, TO, or judge reading this:** feedback, disagreement, and corrections are actively wanted before this goes any further. The goal is a resource the community actually agrees on, not one person's preferences presented as settled guidance.

**For stores that want to try it anyway in its current draft form**, suggested actions:

1. Complete the Store Addendum (Section 6) together with your judges, adjusting numbers as needed.
2. Identify and certify at least one backup TO (in addition to the owner, if applicable).
3. Certify at least one staff member with the appropriate certification for scorekeeping duties under current policy.
4. Share the completed Addendum with all judges who work events at the store.
5. Revisit the Addendum whenever compensation format or event lineup changes.

## 10. Related Policy Suggestion Submitted to Play! Pokémon

Separately from this proposal, a support request has been submitted to Play! Pokémon requesting clarification of Tournament Rules Handbook §3.3.5 (the scorekeeper certification requirement is currently unnamed in the text), and suggesting that League Assistants be permitted to fill the Scorekeeper role for League-level events (League Nights, Challenges, Cups) without requiring full Organizer certification — while leaving the Organizer-certification requirement in place for Regionals, International Championships, and Worlds. This is a policy suggestion to TPCi, not something this guideline can grant on its own; Section 4's scorekeeper requirement reflects current actual policy unless and until that changes.
